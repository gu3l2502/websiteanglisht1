📍 Must-Visit Destinations

Berat: The Stone Heritage

This refers to Berat, one of the oldest and most picturesque cities in the country, recognized by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site. The nickname comes from its distinctive Ottoman-era houses, which are built closely together on a steep hillside, creating the illusion of hundreds of windows stacked on top of each other. The historic Berat Castle still has residents living inside its walls, which is extremely rare for such ancient fortresses. The city’s history spans over 2,400 years, with influences from Illyrian, Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman civilizations. Inside the castle, you’ll find churches, mosques, and the museum dedicated to Onufri, a famous icon painter known for his unique use of red tones.

The Ionian Riviera

The stretch from Dhërmi to Ksamil is part of the Albanian Riviera, often considered one of the most beautiful coastal regions in the Balkans. Dhërmi is known for its crystal-clear water, white pebbled beaches, and lively summer atmosphere. Along the way, you’ll pass hidden gems like Gjipe Canyon, a stunning canyon that leads to a secluded beach. Ksamil is famous for its small islands that you can actually swim to, as well as its turquoise, almost tropical-looking waters.

The Albanian Alps

The villages of Theth and Valbona Valley are located in the dramatic Albanian Alps, also known as the “Accursed Mountains.” These areas offer some of the best hiking trails in Europe, especially the famous Theth–Valbona trail, which connects the two valleys through breathtaking mountain passes. The region is known for its untouched nature, traditional stone houses, and rich local culture. A must-see natural attraction is the Blue Eye of Theth, a deep, icy-blue spring surrounded by forest.

🗺️ Travel Information

History & Castles

Krujë is one of Albania’s most important historical cities, closely linked to the national hero Skanderbeg. The Krujë Castle served as Skanderbeg’s stronghold during his resistance against the Ottoman Empire. The old bazaar nearby is a great place to experience traditional crafts and souvenirs. In the north, Rozafa Castle in Shkodër stands on a hill surrounded by three rivers. It is famous for the legend of a woman named Rozafa, who was walled into the castle to ensure its strength. Albania’s history overall reflects a blend of Illyrian, Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman influences.

Tirana: The Heartbeat

Tirana, the capital, is known for its vibrant energy and colorful architecture. Skanderbeg Square is the heart of the city, surrounded by important landmarks. The Bunk'Art museums are former communist bunkers transformed into immersive historical exhibitions. Tirana is also famous for its nightlife, cafés, and a youthful atmosphere that makes it one of the most dynamic cities in the region.

🤝 "Besa" & Hospitality

"The house of the Albanian belongs to God and the guest." This ancient code of honor, Besa, led Albanians to save every Jewish refugee during WWII. It is the foundation of our identity.

🍽️ Traditional Gastronomy

Tavë Kosi & Byrek

Albanian cuisine is known for being fresh, seasonal, and often organic, with many ingredients sourced locally from farms and mountains. One of the most iconic dishes is Tavë Kosi, made with tender lamb baked in a creamy mixture of yogurt and eggs—simple but incredibly rich in flavor. Another traditional specialty is Flija, prepared by slowly baking thin layers of batter over an open fire, usually served with butter or yogurt. Meals are often accompanied by fresh vegetables, olive oil, homemade bread, and local cheeses, reflecting a Mediterranean influence mixed with Balkan traditions.

Coffee Rituals

In Albania, coffee is much more than just a drink—it’s a daily ritual and a social experience. Sitting at cafés for hours is completely normal, especially in cities like Tirana. People meet to talk, relax, discuss business, or simply enjoy each other’s company. Whether it’s an espresso or traditional Turkish-style coffee, the focus is on connection rather than rushing—reflecting strong social and community bonds.

🎭 Arts & Heritage

Folk Costumes (Xhubleta)

The Xhubleta is one of the oldest traditional garments in Europe, with origins dating back over 4,000 years. It is a bell-shaped dress traditionally worn by women in northern Albania. The design, patterns, and colors vary by region and often symbolize social status, marital status, or local identity. It is officially recognized and protected by UNESCO as an important element of intangible cultural heritage.

Iso-Polyphony

Southern Albanian singing, known as Albanian Iso-Polyphony, is a unique musical tradition performed without instruments. It involves multiple voices singing in harmony, including a lead singer, a drone (iso), and supporting voices. The result is a powerful and emotional sound that can feel almost haunting. This tradition is also recognized by UNESCO for its cultural importance and uniqueness.
